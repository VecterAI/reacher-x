_Example post for reviewing the blog design. The scenario and publication date are illustrative._

## Look at the misses first

For this fictional review, a team reads a small batch of results and records why each one was useful or irrelevant. The numbers below are made up to demonstrate table formatting.

| Search                | Found | Reviewed | Useful | Still waiting | Main source of false matches                                         |
| --------------------- | ----: | -------: | -----: | ------------: | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Follow-up reminders   | 1,280 |       24 |      8 |            16 | People discussing personal calendars rather than sales conversations |
| Shared customer notes |    96 |       18 |      6 |             0 | Job advertisements that mention a CRM                                |
| Spreadsheet handoffs  |    42 |       12 |      3 |             2 | General spreadsheet tutorials without a stated problem               |
| A new phrase          |     0 |        0 |      — |             0 | No conversations collected yet                                       |

### Read the explanation behind each count

A larger result set can contain more repetition. Compare a few conversations from each search before deciding to expand it.

> A small list of relevant conversations is easier to review than a long list with no explanation for why each person is there.

## Decide what to change

| Observation                                      |      Next step      | Owner |
| :----------------------------------------------- | :-----------------: | ----: |
| Results include job advertisements               |  Add an exclusion   |  Maya |
| A useful phrase appears in several conversations |  Test that phrase   |   Sam |
| There are no results for a new search            | Revisit the wording |   Lee |

The right-aligned owner column is included to show how alignment behaves with words as well as numbers.

<BlogCallout title="Do not read these as product metrics">

This is sample data for inspecting the blog UI. It is not a benchmark, a customer result, or a claim about ReacherX performance.

</BlogCallout>

---

## Keep the next review small

1. Change one phrase.
2. Read a fresh sample.
3. Write down whether the new results are easier to explain.

That gives the next reviewer something concrete to compare.
