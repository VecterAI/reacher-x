Some things a message says better with a picture attached. In ReacherX you can attach images, GIFs, and videos to the messages you write, and you can hand them to △ Agent, the assistant that drafts outreach, to use in a plan. A plan is the prepared approach for one person, including the message and its attachments.

If you're new to the app, [How ReacherX works](/blog/how-reacherx-works) explains the words used here, such as workspace, person, and plan. This post builds on those, so a quick read first will help.

You still need to explain what the person is looking at. A video with "thoughts?" next to it isn't much of an introduction.

## Show the part they asked about

Say someone in your workspace has posted that collecting feedback from clients is taking too much time. If your app helps with that, record the part where the client leaves feedback. They don't need a tour of every feature.

For design work, a before-and-after screenshot might explain more than a long description. For a bug, show the steps in a short recording. Use whichever makes the point easier to understand.

Hide private details and unrelated tabs before sharing. Add captions if the recording needs speech to make sense.

## Attach it and say what it's for

Choose the file in the message composer, which is the panel where you write and send a reply, and add a sentence. For the feedback example:

> This clip shows how a client can leave feedback without creating an account.

If you're asking △ Agent to prepare outreach, attach the file to your request and explain when to use it:

> Use this demo in the first message to the designers I selected. It shows the client feedback screen. Keep the message short.

Then check the draft and the attachment. Platform formats and file limits change often, so the composer is the source of truth for the current restrictions.

## Can I reuse the same demo?

Yes. You can include it when asking for [plans for several people](/blog/create-plans-for-several-people). Give △ Agent any instructions that differ from person to person, then read the resulting messages.

The file can be the same, but why you're sharing it may be different. One person might have asked about client accounts. Another might care about collecting comments in one place. Explain the relevant part.

For something you'd rather say out loud, you can also [send your own voice note](/blog/send-voice-notes) in a supported conversation.
