Sometimes you know exactly what data you want: the people who commented on a particular post, for example. Sometimes you only know the outcome: find people who would benefit from a workshop you're organizing.

PhantomBuster and ReacherX can both help with finding and reaching people. Their starting points are worth understanding before you choose.

## PhantomBuster gives you specific automations

[PhantomBuster](https://phantombuster.com/) offers prebuilt workflows for gathering social data, enriching lists, and running outreach. Its current product includes live signals, AI-assisted personalization, and LinkedIn messaging.

If your job is to collect a particular set of people or repeat a known data operation, start by checking its automation catalog. Look at the required input, output, account connection, and schedule for the exact automation you need.

That approach can fit a process you've already designed, especially when the data needs to move into other tools.

## ReacherX starts with the people you need

In ReacherX, describe the goal and audience. For a workshop, that might be:

> Find independent designers who have recently talked about difficult client feedback. I'm organizing a small free workshop on keeping feedback organized. I want people who are dealing with this now.

ReacherX turns the audience into searches, checks possible matches, gathers more information, and can prepare plans for relevant people. You can read a profile and the supporting posts, then work on the invitation and continue supported X/Twitter or LinkedIn conversations in the app.

You don't need to choose and connect a set of automations to use this hosted workflow. You do need to review the proposed audience and tell △ Agent when it misunderstood you.

## Which parts do you want to configure?

| What matters to you?                                     | What to compare                                                             |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A known extraction or enrichment task                    | PhantomBuster's exact automation inputs and outputs                         |
| Finding people from a plain-language project description | ReacherX's setup, discovery, and fit checks                                 |
| Bringing data into an existing process                   | PhantomBuster's exports and integrations                                    |
| Keeping research, plans, and conversations together      | ReacherX's profiles and supported messaging                                 |
| Hiring, research, and community projects                 | ReacherX's use cases and separate workspace instructions                    |
| Using an external AI assistant                           | PhantomBuster has a documented MCP server; ReacherX's MCP server is planned |

PhantomBuster's [MCP documentation](https://hub.phantombuster.com/docs/mcp-server) explains access from assistants such as Claude and ChatGPT. ReacherX should not get credit for a planned feature that another tool already offers.

## What makes ReacherX worth trying

Its appeal is the combination of a guided starting point and ongoing work around each person. You can save instructions, prepare individual plans for a selection of people, review messages, and inspect search activity and memories.

The application is also open source under Apache-2.0. Developers can run it and change its behavior. That requires development work and service configuration; ordinary users can use the hosted app without doing that.

ReacherX is still beta. It doesn't offer every extraction source or integration in PhantomBuster's catalog. If a particular source is essential, verify it before switching.

## Test the complete job

Use the same real goal when evaluating both. Check whether the results fit, how you find out why they were selected, and what work remains before you can have a useful conversation.

If you already have a reliable automation process, replacing it may not help. If you're piecing together tools just to find a few relevant people, [ReacherX's first-use guide](/blog/getting-started-with-reacherx) shows the alternative workflow.
