ReacherX finds people on X/Twitter and LinkedIn who fit a description you write, then helps you reach them. This guide is about creators, the people who make videos, tutorials, or posts for an audience. The goal here is a collaboration that makes sense for both sides, not a paid post from the biggest name you can find.

A creator with a large audience isn't automatically the right person for your product. The useful question is whether their viewers would care about what you make. If you're building a developer tool, a small creator who regularly teaches the exact problem you solve may be worth a closer look.

If the product's words are new to you, [How ReacherX works](/blog/how-reacherx-works) explains workspace, person, plan, and the rest in a few minutes of reading. The short version is that a workspace holds one job, and each person ReacherX finds is saved there with the posts and background that explain the match.

## Search for the topic they cover

Try a request like this in ReacherX.

> Find creators on X/Twitter and LinkedIn who teach solo developers how to launch web apps. I'm looking for people who share hands-on tutorials, not just general startup advice.

Then open their work. What do they explain? Who asks questions in the replies? Do they review products, teach with them, or mainly share opinions?

Those details should influence what you ask them to do. A tutorial creator and an interviewer may need very different proposals.

## Give them a useful way to try it

If you're asking a creator to cover your app, suggest the slot it fits. The next step in a tutorial they already teach, or a problem their audience keeps asking about, both work.

You could write something like this.

> Your tutorial on launching a side project made me think our tool might be useful for the next step, finding people to try it. Would you like to test it? I can send a short walkthrough.

Use [a demo](/blog/outreach-with-images-and-video) that shows the part their audience would understand. Be clear about whether you're offering payment, product access, or simply asking for feedback. Don't leave them to guess whether this is a paid project.

When you've picked someone, ask △ Agent, the AI agent that runs your workspace, to prepare a plan for them. A plan lays out the messages and steps for the approach. With sending approvals on, the default, you can change any part of it before it runs; [autonomous mode](/blog/what-reacherx-does-automatically) can send supported messages without another approval.

## Agree on the work before it starts

Discuss the format, timing, payment, and what they are free to say about the product. If they've tried it and don't like it, that's useful feedback too.

Details like that are worth keeping. If a creator tells you they only take on sponsored work in certain months, ask △ Agent to remember it. Notes like that become part of the workspace's memory and shape later drafts there.
