“Frontend engineer” covers a lot of different work. Someone who builds landing pages may not want a job untangling a large application's keyboard navigation.

Before looking for your first hire, write down what you actually need help with. It will make both the search and the first conversation easier.

## Search for the work, not just the title

You can ask ReacherX:

> Help me find frontend engineers on X and LinkedIn who have shared work on accessibility or complex web apps. We're a small team hiring someone to improve our product's UI. They need to work within three hours of our time zone.

Change those details to match the real role. If a requirement is optional, say so. You don't want a useful person excluded because you listed every nice-to-have as essential.

Open the results and look at what they've made or written. Was it their project? What did they contribute? Would you want to ask them about it in an interview?

## Give them the role details early

A message like “Exciting opportunity, interested?” asks someone to reply before telling them anything useful.

Try an opening that explains why you're contacting them:

> I read your post about making that settings screen work with a keyboard. We're hiring someone to do similar work on our app. It's a remote role on a team of three. Can I send you the details?

Share pay, hours, and responsibilities when you can. Those details help someone decide whether the conversation is worth their time.

## Keep track of the people you want to speak with

ReacherX's recruiting workspace uses candidates and hiring stages, so you can keep your shortlist organized. You can [prepare separate plans for several candidates](/blog/create-plans-for-several-people) and [continue their DMs in the app](/blog/manage-dm-conversations).

The search helps you find people worth speaking with. Getting to know them, discussing the role, and deciding whether to work together still takes a real conversation.
