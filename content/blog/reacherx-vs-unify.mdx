Being able to type a request doesn't tell you what an AI product is good at. You need to look at the work it can finish.

Unify and ReacherX both let you give an agent instructions. The useful comparison starts with the result you're after.

## Give Unify a sales job

[Unify](https://www.unifygtm.com/) describes a chat-based outbound agent for sellers. Its site includes list building, account research, contact enrichment, sequencing, and Salesforce and HubSpot integrations. It shows examples of using network connections and recent LinkedIn activity to prepare outreach.

So the distinction isn't that Unify needs an engineer while ReacherX accepts plain language. Unify explicitly aims to reduce that setup work too.

If you have a sales team working accounts, ask it to handle a representative account. Check the research, the contact data, and what would happen when the work reaches your existing CRM.

## Give ReacherX a people problem

Suppose your week includes finding a product researcher, inviting two potential workshop partners, and looking for customers. Each job needs a different definition of a good match.

ReacherX provides use-case workspaces for this. A partnership workspace can focus on mutual value and audience overlap. Recruiting uses relevant experience and role fit. The names and progress stages change as well as △ Agent's context.

You describe the requirements, review people found on X/Twitter and LinkedIn, and work on individual outreach plans. Profiles, supported DMs, saved instructions, and reporting sit around that process. You can keep hiring separate from sales with different instructions for each.

## Check the channel before the clever part

ReacherX is still in beta and does not yet send email through Gmail or Outlook. If a coordinated email sequence is central to your process, examine Unify's current engagement features before choosing ReacherX.

If your approach begins with someone's public work on X/Twitter or LinkedIn, try ReacherX with a precise audience. Ask it to explain why each result belongs, then prepare one introduction. You should be able to correct the approach without rebuilding an automation.

## Ask what △ Agent learned from your correction

In ReacherX, a saved workspace instruction can guide later work, and the system remembers your corrections so they can shape future plans. That's remembered context, not model retraining, and no correction guarantees a better reply every time.

Test a concrete instruction such as “Ask about their experience before inviting them to a demo.” Inspect the next plan to see whether that direction is reflected. The [contact-management guide](/blog/manage-people-with-reacherx) explains how the resulting work stays organized.
