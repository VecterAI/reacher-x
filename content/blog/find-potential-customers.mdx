ReacherX finds people on X/Twitter and LinkedIn who match a description you write, then helps you start a conversation with them. For your first customers, that means looking for people already talking about the problem your product solves. Their posts can tell you more than a job title or company size ever could.

New to ReacherX? [How ReacherX works](/blog/how-reacherx-works) explains the words used here, like workspace, plan, and memory. The short version for this guide is that a workspace holds one job, and each person it finds is saved there with the posts that explain the match.

## Describe the problem in the customer's words

Suppose your app helps freelance designers collect feedback from clients. "Find customers for my SaaS" doesn't tell ReacherX much.

Try something like:

> Find freelance designers who have talked about clients sending feedback through email, chat, and shared documents. I'm building an app that puts that feedback in one place.

You can add things that matter, such as a country or the size of the business. Leave out restrictions that don't affect whether you can help.

## Read the conversation before offering anything

Open a promising result. What exactly was the person struggling with? Have they already found an answer? Did someone suggest an approach they've tried before?

A complaint about feedback doesn't automatically mean they want another app. Maybe they need a better process, or their client refuses to use anything new.

That's useful to learn before you spend time writing a pitch. Note what you hear, too. You can save a lesson like that as a workspace memory, and later searches and drafts in the same workspace take it into account.

## Send something they can respond to

For the designer example, an opening could be:

> You mentioned getting feedback in three different places. Does the main problem happen when you're collecting it, or when you're trying to work out which changes the client has approved?

Ask because you want to understand the answer. If your app helps, explain how. [A short demo](/blog/outreach-with-images-and-video) can make that easier.

When you've found a few people worth writing to, you can [prepare plans for a few people](/blog/create-plans-for-several-people), then check each message before sending. A plan is the set of draft messages for one person, and △ Agent, the AI agent in your workspace, prepares it from what it knows about them. Start small enough that you can follow the replies yourself.
