“Let's partner” is a hard message to answer. Partner on what? How much work will it take? Why would it help?

It gets easier when you have a small project in mind. A workshop, a useful integration, or a guest session gives the other person something they can picture.

## Work out what you could do together

Suppose you make a tool for freelance designers and want to run a workshop on client feedback. Someone teaching freelance design could be a good person to speak with.

Ask ReacherX:

> Find people on X and LinkedIn who teach freelance designers or run communities for them. I'd like to run a practical workshop about handling client feedback. Look for people whose recent work relates to that topic.

Open the results and read what they teach. You want a reason their audience would care, not just a large follower count.

## Suggest a first project

Instead of asking for an open-ended partnership, explain the idea:

> I liked your lesson on setting expectations with clients. Would a short workshop on handling feedback be useful for your members? I can prepare the examples and run the session.

Adapt that to what you can actually offer. Say what you'd do and what you'd need from them. If there are costs, fees, or shared revenue to discuss, bring those up before either of you starts working.

A one-page outline or [short video](/blog/outreach-with-images-and-video) can help them judge the idea quickly.

## Keep the first version small

You don't need a long-term agreement to find out whether you work well together. One useful session can be enough to learn what to try next.

ReacherX can help you [prepare plans for a few potential partners](/blog/create-plans-for-several-people), each with a different proposal. Once someone replies, shape the project around that conversation rather than sending the next message from an old draft.
