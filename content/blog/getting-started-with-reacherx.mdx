ReacherX finds people you might want to contact, such as candidates, customers, or collaborators, and helps you prepare the first message. This guide walks through your first hour, from setup to your first approved plan.

Four words carry most of what follows. A workspace is the container for one search, holding the people the system finds, the plans for contacting them, and the instructions you give. A person is anyone the system saves into that workspace as a possible match. A plan is the prepared approach for one person, including the message. △ Agent is the assistant that runs the searches, drafts the plans, and asks for your approval. [How ReacherX works](/blog/how-reacherx-works) explains these terms in more depth if you want the full picture before you start.

You do need to explain what you want, though. ReacherX won't know what makes someone useful to you until you tell it. For this walkthrough, we'll use hiring. You need an engineer for a contract project that involves payments in a web app.

## Go through setup with one goal in mind

Setup starts with a conversation. You describe the work and the experience the person needs, and △ Agent uses that request to propose an audience and example candidates. An audience is the description of who counts as a match, written as requirements the system can check.

You could start with:

> I need an engineer for a contract project. They should have experience building checkout or payment features for web apps. Help me find people whose work shows that experience. I want to ask whether they're available for a conversation about the project.

Be clear about requirements and preferences. If a particular technology is required, say so. Add your website if it helps explain the project. Read the proposed audience before continuing and correct anything △ Agent misunderstood.

Review the example candidates and choose Continue when they fit. You'll need a connected X/Twitter or LinkedIn account and a paid plan before results can run. If you already have both, setup takes you straight to the workspace. The search you're setting up lives in that workspace. [This guide explains workspaces](/blog/workspaces-explained) and how to keep different kinds of searches separate.

## Read the first results

Each person saved into the workspace arrives with evidence behind them, which is the record of what the system actually saw, such as posts, titles, and activity. Open someone's profile and look at why they matched. For this example, does the evidence show work on payments, or just a general engineering job title? It won't necessarily tell you whether they're available for a contract. That's something you may need to ask.

Tell △ Agent what missed the point. Ask it to update the audience and review the change. If the workspace needs attention, open its status and read the reason. An unfinished setup or a disconnected account needs fixing before asking for more results.

For a person you'd like to contact, ask for a plan. Read the proposed message and change what doesn't fit. Keep approvals on while you learn the app. That's the default. [Autonomous mode](/blog/what-reacherx-does-automatically) can send supported messages without another approval once you turn it on.

## Save what you want it to remember

If you have an instruction for every introduction, ask △ Agent to remember it for this workspace. Memory is the instruction the agent saves and reuses in its later work here. For example, you might always want to mention the contract length. Check that it confirms the instruction was saved.

On your next visit, read replies and work waiting for approval. Update anything that happened outside the app. [Analytics](/blog/read-your-reacherx-analytics) is there when you want to look at the wider picture.
