A useful targeting brief tells ReacherX who matters to you and why. You don't need a long prompt. You need enough detail to distinguish a promising match from a person who only shares a job title.

## Start with the outcome

Describe what you want to achieve. Finding customers, interviewing research participants, and inviting podcast guests call for different evidence.

For example: “I want to interview people who manage customer support at small software companies.”

## Make the audience specific

Include the details that actually change your decision:

- **Role:** who owns the problem or makes the decision?
- **Context:** what kind of organization or community are they part of?
- **Relevant signal:** what activity or experience suggests a fit?
- **Exclusions:** who should be left out?

| Broad description | More useful description                                          |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| People in SaaS    | Founders of small B2B software companies                         |
| Marketing leaders | People responsible for a developer product’s community           |
| Podcast guests    | Engineers who have shared lessons from running production agents |

## Try a complete brief

Copy this example and change it to reflect your own audience:

```text
I want to find founders of small B2B software companies
who have recently talked about customer onboarding.

Prioritize people who describe a specific onboarding problem.
Exclude agencies selling onboarding services.

My goal is to invite them to a short research interview.
```

<BlogCallout title="Use observable signals">

“Recently discussed an onboarding problem” is easier to evaluate than “would love my product.” Give the agent evidence to look for, and review the evidence it finds.

</BlogCallout>

## Review and refine

Look at a few results before expanding the search. If the matches are too broad, add one meaningful constraint. If they are too narrow, remove a constraint that isn't essential.
