## Start with what the person said

A public question can be narrow: someone wants to remember a follow-up, find an old note, or understand why a workflow keeps breaking. A long introduction to a product may answer a different question entirely.

The useful first step is usually smaller. Read the wording. Look at the replies already there. Notice what remains unanswered.

> I do not need a complete system yet. I need a way to remember which conversation I should return to tomorrow.
>
> If the suggestion takes longer to set up than the task itself, I am unlikely to try it.

This is a fictional quote, included to show how a quotation with more than one paragraph looks.

### Ask one question that changes your answer

“What have you tried?” can help, but a more specific question may be easier to answer: _Are you working alone, or do other people need to see the same notes?_

That distinction matters. A private reminder and a shared handoff are different jobs.

> A reviewer’s note:
>
> > The reply should address the question before introducing the product.
>
> Keep the suggestion short enough that the person can decide whether it fits.

## Leave room for a different answer

**Confidence in the wording is not evidence that the suggestion is right.** A reply can be clear and still miss the person’s situation. Read a second time before sending it.[^review]

Replace ~~a full product introduction~~ a paragraph the person did not ask for with one useful suggestion and, when needed, one question.
