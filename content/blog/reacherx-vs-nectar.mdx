I want to build a community around ReacherX. I want people to try it, tell me what needs work, and help build it. Getting them into a Discord server is only the beginning. I want them to have a reason to stay.

That is one use for ReacherX: finding people who might care about what you're doing. Nectar also works with communities, but its offer includes much more of the work around a brand's social conversations.

## If conversations are already coming in

[Nectar](https://www.nectarsocial.com/) describes a social platform for brand conversations, listening, creators, community management, and commerce. Its offer includes AI replies, configurable workflows, conversation classification, and reporting across channels. Its community hub builds member profiles that surface super-fans, and it syncs social conversations to revenue attribution.

You might need to answer a product question, handle a complaint, or follow up with a creator. Those conversations need different responses. In a Nectar demo, bring examples from your own accounts. Ask to see how it sorts them, prepares replies, and hands work to a person. Check which channels are included in the offer you would buy.

## If you still need to meet the people

For a new group, you may have nobody to reply to yet. Say you want to bring together engineers who work on accessibility. Where do you find them? What would make the group useful to them?

Tell ReacherX who you're looking for and why. When the description is about bringing people into a group, ReacherX sets the workspace up for community outreach. △ Agent looks on X/Twitter and LinkedIn, checks possible matches, and gathers background. Open a profile and read the person's work. A matching job title alone doesn't tell you whether they'll care about your group.

When you find someone relevant, ask for an outreach plan and continue supported conversations from their profile; unrelated searches stay separate in their own workspaces ([more on that](/blog/manage-people-with-reacherx)).

## Give people a reason to care

Before asking anyone to join, decide what you can offer. For that accessibility group, perhaps it's a monthly discussion where members bring problems from their work. Put that in your instructions. △ Agent needs something more useful than “invite people to my amazing community.”

Read the proposed invitation as if you were receiving it. Does it explain why this person was contacted? Is there something they would want to take part in? Save corrections as workspace instructions. Sometimes replying to their work is a better start than sending an invitation.

△ Agent can help with the introduction. You still have to make the community worth joining.

## Where the choice becomes clear

I'd look at Nectar when there's social activity to manage across a brand's channels. Try ReacherX when you need help finding people and approaching them; its v4 beta outreach runs on X/Twitter and LinkedIn.

Start with the guide to [finding community members](/blog/find-community-members), or [finding creators](/blog/find-creators) if you're looking for collaborators. I build ReacherX and haven't tested these products against each other; Nectar's site was checked September 10, 2026. Check its current offer and [ReacherX pricing](/pricing) before paying.
