“Find people, contact them, and improve over time” describes more than one product. Gigamultiplier is one of them, so it deserves more than a checkbox comparison.

The useful questions are which people you can look for, what △ Agent does with a reply, and how you steer the work.

## There is real overlap

[Gigamultiplier, from Gigacatalyst](https://gigacatalyst.com/multiplier), describes finding people from a plain-language request and reaching them on LinkedIn. Its page includes investors and alumni as well as customers, and says replies and meetings inform later adjustments.

That means ReacherX cannot fairly claim that broader audiences or adaptive outreach exist only here. If those are the features you want, put both workflows to the test.

One real difference sits in the sending mechanics. Gigamultiplier sends LinkedIn requests from your own connected account through residential proxies near your location, at randomized times of day, and its FAQ says nothing goes out until you approve the buyers and the sequence. It also advertises a refund of the first month's base fee if your first paid month books no qualified meeting.

The Gigamultiplier page also describes working from Sales Navigator or an uploaded CSV. Check which of those options your plan actually includes before connecting anything.

## What to inspect in ReacherX

Start with one purpose, such as finding integration partners. Explain what your product does, what you can contribute, and what would make the partnership unsuitable.

ReacherX searches supported sources on X/Twitter and LinkedIn, qualifies possible matches, and gathers background. Open a profile and check whether the evidence supports a partnership conversation. Ask △ Agent for a plan only after that makes sense to you.

A separate workspace can hold hiring or investor work. Those workspaces have their own audience, plans, saved instructions, and reporting. The use case ReacherX assigns from your description changes both the visible stages and the context given to △ Agent.

## Make the adaptation visible

A system saying it improves isn't the same as being able to inspect a change. In ReacherX, explicit preferences can become workspace memories. New replies or interactions can lead △ Agent to continue, finish, or stop an active plan.

Try giving a correction with a clear purpose: “Don't offer an integration until we've confirmed they serve the same kind of customer.” Inspect the next proposed steps and the relevant saved instruction. [The memory guide](/blog/teach-reacherx-what-you-want) explains what to ask for.

Keep review-required sending enabled while you do this. Autonomous mode can send supported messages without another approval, and △ Agent can still misunderstand an event.

## Judge a conversation, not a promise

Pick one audience and review a small number of results. Can you explain why each person belongs? Does the proposed introduction offer something relevant? When a person declines, can you stop the work and keep their status accurate?

I build ReacherX, which is open source and still in beta; Gigamultiplier's page was checked September 10, 2026, and this article doesn't verify its account-protection or conversion claims. Weigh both against [ReacherX pricing](/pricing) for the work you need done.
