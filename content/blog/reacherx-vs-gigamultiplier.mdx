“Find people, contact them, and improve over time” describes more than one product. Gigamultiplier is one of them, so it deserves more than a checkbox comparison.

The useful questions are which people you can look for, what the agent does with a reply, and how you steer the work.

## There is real overlap

[Gigamultiplier, from Gigacatalyst](https://gigacatalyst.com/multiplier), describes finding people from a plain-language request and reaching them on LinkedIn. Its page includes investors and alumni as well as customers, and says replies and meetings inform later adjustments.

That means ReacherX cannot fairly claim that broader audiences or adaptive outreach exist only here. If those are the features you want, put both workflows to the test.

The Gigamultiplier page also describes working from Sales Navigator or an uploaded CSV. Confirm the current access and onboarding terms directly rather than assuming every path is included in a trial.

## What to inspect in ReacherX

Start with one purpose, such as finding integration partners. Explain what your product does, what you can contribute, and what would make the partnership unsuitable.

ReacherX searches supported sources on X and LinkedIn, qualifies possible matches, and gathers background. Open a profile and check whether the evidence supports a partnership conversation. Ask the agent for a plan only after that makes sense to you.

A separate workspace can hold hiring or investor work. Those workspaces have their own audience, plans, saved instructions, and reporting. The selected use case changes both the visible stages and the context given to the agent.

## Make the adaptation visible

A system saying it improves isn't the same as being able to inspect a change. In ReacherX, explicit preferences can become workspace memories. New replies or interactions can lead the agent to continue, finish, or stop an active plan.

Try giving a correction with a clear purpose: “Don't offer an integration until we've confirmed they serve the same kind of customer.” Inspect the next proposed steps and the relevant saved instruction. [The memory guide](/blog/teach-reacherx-what-you-want) explains what to ask for.

Keep review-required sending enabled while you do this. Autonomous mode can send supported messages without another approval, and an agent can still misunderstand an event.

## Judge a conversation, not a promise

Pick one audience and review a small number of results. Can you explain why each person belongs? Does the proposed introduction offer something relevant? When a person declines, can you stop the work and keep their status accurate?

ReacherX v4 remains beta. Its code is open source, and its current supported outreach is on X and LinkedIn. Email sending and calendar integrations are planned.
