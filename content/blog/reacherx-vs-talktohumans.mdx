Your LinkedIn inbox is busy, but the conversation you meant to continue has disappeared under everything else. That is a different problem from having no relevant people to contact.

TalkToHumans and ReacherX meet around one-to-one conversations. Their starting points and sending controls deserve a closer look.

## If the inbox is the problem

[TalkToHumans](https://www.talktohumans.app/) describes a LinkedIn inbox with custom views, tags, tailored sequences, contact management, and CRM integrations. It also offers MCP access on a listed plan.

Its public page says LinkedIn actions run through your browser and that AI can research and draft, but cannot send messages or connection requests on your behalf. Check how follow-ups behave in a trial; a scheduled follow-up and an AI deciding to send are different kinds of automation.

If you're already having many LinkedIn conversations, test whether those inbox views help you find the ones that need attention. A dedicated view for a real task is more useful than simply seeing all your messages at once.

## If you still need to find the people

ReacherX begins with your goal. You might need an interview participant, a potential partner, or a candidate. Describe what makes someone suitable, then review possible matches from X and LinkedIn.

The agent can qualify and enrich those people, prepare individual outreach plans, and help revise the approach. You can open a person's profile to inspect the plan and continue a supported chat.

There is contact management around that work: stages that fit the use case, separate workspaces, and saved instructions. You can also prepare plans for several selected people without giving them all the same message.

## Choose how much sending you want to hand over

ReacherX starts with review-required sending. Autonomous mode can run supported outreach without another approval and can start existing draft plans after the setting's confirmation. If you want to stay involved in each send, keep the review setting enabled.

This is a material choice, not just a convenience switch. Try rewriting a proposed message, declining a step, and seeing where the work waits for you. Read [the automation guide](/blog/what-reacherx-does-automatically) before changing the default.

## Walk through one real conversation

Pick a person whose reply needs a thoughtful answer. In each tool, find the context, prepare your reply, check the sender, and work out what happens next. Then ask whether finding the next relevant person is part of the same job for you.

ReacherX v4 is still beta, and its unified inbox is planned. Today's DMs live within individual profiles. If centralized LinkedIn inbox management is your immediate requirement, give that difference weight. You can see the current ReacherX flow in [the DM guide](/blog/manage-dm-conversations).
