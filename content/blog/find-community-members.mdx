This guide is about a job I have myself. I open-sourced ReacherX because I want to meet people who are excited about the things I'm excited about. People I can ask questions, exchange ideas with, and build with. ReacherX finds people on X/Twitter and LinkedIn who fit a description you write, and a community is one of the things you can look for.

Getting a number next to the member count isn't the goal. I want people who actually talk to each other. If the vocabulary is new to you, a workspace is where ReacherX keeps one job, with its own people, messages, and saved lessons. [How ReacherX works](/blog/how-reacherx-works) gives a short tour of that idea and the other words used here.

## Who would you like to meet?

Be specific when you set up a community workspace in ReacherX. For example, you could be starting a group where solo developers try each other's apps. Tell △ Agent, the AI agent that runs your searches and drafts, what you're looking for:

> Find solo developers on X/Twitter and LinkedIn who are sharing early versions of their apps and asking for feedback. I want to bring them together for a weekly session where we try each other's projects.

That gives it an audience and a reason to look for them. "Find people for my Discord" doesn't tell it much.

Read the posts it finds. If someone is asking for feedback you can give, help them there. They shouldn't have to join your server first.

When ReacherX finds a possible member, it saves that person in your workspace together with the evidence behind the match, such as their posts and bio. Reading that evidence is how you decide whether someone would care about your group.

## What are you inviting them to?

I wouldn't know what to do with "join our amazing community" if I received it. What happens there? Is there something I'd want to be part of?

For the developer group, an invitation could be:

> I saw you're looking for feedback on the onboarding. We're doing a small project feedback session this Friday. Would you like to bring your app?

Only say that if you've actually arranged the session. Include the time, where it happens, and any cost. A small group is fine. You don't need to make it sound bigger than it is.

When you're ready to invite people, you can ask ReacherX for [plans for several people](/blog/create-plans-for-several-people). A plan is the approach prepared for one person, with draft messages. With sending approvals on, the default, nothing goes out until you have reviewed it. Read each invitation. If it doesn't explain why you thought of that person, it needs work.

## Be there when they arrive

Answer their question. Try their project. Introduce them to someone when there's a reason to. Those are the things I want from a community myself.

If you find yourself correcting △ Agent often, remember that corrections stick. ReacherX keeps lessons like that as workspace memories, so the same mistake doesn't come back next week.
