A guest who has just done the thing you want to discuss often beats the most famous name in the field; the story is still fresh for them, and for your listeners.

Start from the episode idea, then look for the person who can make the conversation worth listening to.

## Search around the episode

For an episode about going from freelance work to a small agency, you could ask ReacherX:

> Find people on X/Twitter and LinkedIn who have shared their experience hiring their first teammate after freelancing. I'm looking for a podcast guest who can talk about what changed and what went wrong.

Read what the results have shared. Is there enough to build a conversation around? Can you think of two or three questions you'd want to ask this person?

If you can't, narrow the episode idea before reaching out. A narrower idea also makes the invitation easier to write.

## Put the useful details in the invitation

Explain the topic, format, and time involved. For example:

> I host a podcast for freelancers. Your post about hiring your first designer raised a question I'd like to explore: how do you hand over client work without reviewing every detail? Would you be open to a 30-minute recorded conversation about it?

Add a link to an episode so they can hear what the show is like. Mention whether the recording includes video and where you'll publish it.

You can ask ReacherX to [help prepare the invitation](/blog/create-plans-for-several-people), but keep the question that made you interested in this guest.

## Make saying yes easy

Offer a few possible times and explain what preparation, if any, you need. If they ask about your audience, give an honest answer.
