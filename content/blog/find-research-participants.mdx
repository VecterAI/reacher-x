User research only works when you talk to people who have actually dealt with the problem you're studying. Your friends may be happy to try your app, but if they don't have the problem, their feedback can only take you so far. ReacherX helps you find the right people. It searches X/Twitter and LinkedIn for people who match a description you write, and it saves each person it finds in your workspace with the posts and background that explain the match.

Don't worry about the vocabulary. [How ReacherX works](/blog/how-reacherx-works) introduces each word, from workspace to memory, in plain language.

## Look for a recent experience

Say you want to understand how tutors arrange lessons with parents. You could ask ReacherX.

> Find independent tutors who have talked about arranging lessons, handling cancellations, or chasing confirmations from parents. I'd like to interview people who manage their own schedule.

That gives you something more useful to look for than "people interested in education." △ Agent, the AI agent that runs your workspace, uses the details you give it when it decides who might fit, so be honest about who you need.

Read the original posts. You may discover that the issue you wanted to study isn't the part people find difficult.

## Explain what you're asking them to do

Keep the invitation simple:

> I'm researching how independent tutors arrange lessons with parents. Would you be open to a 20-minute call about how you handle it? This is a research interview, not a product demo.

Include whether you'll pay for their time, whether you want to record, and how you'll use the conversation. Give them those details before they agree.

You can use [a separate outreach plan for each person](/blog/create-plans-for-several-people), but don't let the invitation turn into a sales pitch. A plan is just the prepared approach for one person, with draft messages you review. If you said you're there to learn, stay there to learn.

## Check one thing before booking

Ask a simple question to make sure they're the person you need. "Do you arrange the lessons yourself, or does someone else handle that?"

Avoid asking them to agree that your proposed solution is useful. "Wouldn't an app make this easier?" tells you less than "What happened the last time a parent changed a lesson?"

Use [the conversation](/blog/manage-dm-conversations) to answer questions and arrange the next step. If a participant mentions a detail worth keeping, such as the best time to reach them, ask △ Agent to remember it for this workspace.
