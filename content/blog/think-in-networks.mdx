Every people problem sounds different, but underneath it's usually the same problem.

You want to hire an engineer. Where do you find one who has actually shipped payments? You're raising a round. Which partner has backed companies like yours? You freelance. Where is the next client? You built a product. Who needs it?

Different situations, same shape. The hard part is rarely the message. The hard part is knowing the right people, or getting access to them.

## The network is the asset

There's an old line: your network is your net worth. I keep coming back to it because it survives every tool cycle. Contact databases, scrapers, campaign software: all of them treat people as rows to message. A network is the opposite of a row. It's people plus the reasons they're connected: shared work, shared problems, shared communities.

When you have a real network, opportunities arrive without you hunting for them. When you don't, every search starts from zero. Most outreach tools optimize the message at the end of that process. I care about the beginning: finding the people who should be in your network in the first place.

That's also why I don't think of ReacherX as a sales tool. Sales is one reason to need the right people. Hiring is another. So are raising funds, finding research participants, inviting podcast guests, or starting a community. If you only build for sales, you build for one room in the house.

## What developers taught me about this

Developers are the group I know best, and they do something interesting: when a problem annoys them, they build the fix themselves. That's why the developer ecosystem is so robust. It isn't robust because developers were handed better tools. It's robust because they don't wait for someone else to solve their problems, and the things they build for themselves end up serving everyone.

Finding the right people is a problem developers have too, and I don't believe it gets solved by another enterprise database. I believe it gets solved the way developer problems usually get solved: by someone close to the problem building something simple and putting it out in the open.

That's where ReacherX comes from. I'm a developer and designer, not a salesperson, and I'm building the tool I want to use. A lot of it will feel familiar if you use modern developer tools: plain language instead of campaign tables, plans instead of sequences, autocomplete while you write. I wrote about that influence in [AI should help us talk to people, not fill their inboxes](/blog/ai-and-human-connection).

## Authenticity is the scarce resource

There's more AI-generated outreach than ever, and most of it is bad. In a world where anyone can generate a thousand messages, a message that shows real understanding of your situation stands out. My bet is that the tools that win in this space are the ones that get the balance right: automate the searching and reading, and keep the human judgment where it matters.

## Who this is for right now

ReacherX works best for small and early teams, and for one-person teams. If that's you, the network problem is very real: you don't have a sales team, and you still need customers, candidates, investors, or collaborators. I wrote more about who I'm building for in [what ReacherX is for](/blog/what-reacherx-is-for).

If you remember one thing from this post, make it this: stop asking how to message more people, and start asking who is already out there and what they're saying. That question has better answers every day.
