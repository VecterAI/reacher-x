I don't expect someone to know what a CRM is before using ReacherX. They might be a designer looking for work or a founder trying to hire someone. They need to find the right people, talk to them, and remember what's happening with each person.

That's why ReacherX has contact management. Salesforce goes much further into running a business around customer records. The question is how much of that work you need help with.

## What Salesforce is built to cover

[Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/) offers products for sales, service, and marketing. It has Agentforce for AI agents and a Starter Suite for small businesses.

If you have a sales team, a support team, and a marketing team, they may all need to know what's happened with the same customer. A salesperson needs the account history. Support needs to handle the problem the customer reported. The business needs to know how sales are going.

Bring those needs to a Salesforce demo. Ask which products cover them, what the setup involves, and what the whole thing costs. ReacherX v4 beta won't replace that operation. And Salesforce's small-business offer means company size alone isn't a reason to rule it out.

## The job I'm building ReacherX for

I'm a developer and designer myself. I want ReacherX to be useful to someone working alone or in a small team, even if they've never done sales. Hiring, finding investors, and looking for partners are part of that too.

Suppose you need a designer for a short contract. Tell △ Agent what they'll work on and what experience they need. ReacherX searches X/Twitter and LinkedIn, checks possible matches, and gathers background. Read a profile, ask for a plan to approach that person, and discuss changes before approving it.

You don't have to write code or connect a series of automation tools. You do need to tell it when a match is wrong. “They've only worked on marketing sites; I need experience with checkout flows” gives it something useful to work with.

## Keeping track after the introduction

The plan and supported conversation stay with the person's profile. Move them to the next stage when something happens. The workspace's purpose determines what those stages are called:

- Recruiting: Sourced, Contacted, Interviewing, Hired, Archived.
- Partnerships: Identified, Contacted, Negotiating, Partnered, Archived.

That choice also supplies instructions to △ Agent about who to look for and what you're trying to achieve.

If you agree to hire someone on a phone call, mark them Hired. ReacherX can't know that happened just because it finished an outreach plan. The [CRM guide](/blog/manage-people-with-reacherx) explains this in more detail.

## Which one should you try?

Use a real piece of work to decide. For Salesforce, follow a customer from sales through support in a demo. For ReacherX, try finding a few people for your project and reading their plans.

Check channels before paying. ReacherX outreach is currently on X/Twitter and LinkedIn. Gmail/Outlook sending and calendar integrations are planned.

I build ReacherX and haven't run a benchmark against Salesforce. Its official site was checked September 10, 2026. Compare the relevant Salesforce plan with [ReacherX pricing](/pricing), including the setup your work needs.
