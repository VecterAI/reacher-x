If the message needs to go by email, start with that requirement. An impressive social outreach workflow won't change the channel your recipient expects.

Warmbly and ReacherX are both open-source projects, but they are not interchangeable sending systems.

## What Warmbly is built around

[Warmbly](https://warmbly.com/) describes cold-email campaigns, mailbox warmup, sending, and reply handling. It provides a self-hosted option under Apache 2.0. Its site currently includes a “Cloud is coming soon” notice. Confirm that hosted access is actually live before signing up; a signup button isn't proof of a working cloud account.

If you already have an appropriate email audience and need campaign infrastructure, investigate those capabilities. Read its deployment and sending documentation before running it, and check the mailbox services you will need.

## What ReacherX handles today

ReacherX is still in beta and focuses on finding and reaching people on X/Twitter and LinkedIn. You describe who you need, review possible matches, inspect the available evidence, and work on individual outreach plans with △ Agent.

That work might involve a customer, a candidate, an investor, or a potential podcast guest. Separate workspaces keep the audiences, instructions, people, and reporting apart. Use-case stages help you track what has happened without making every relationship look like a sale.

Within supported conversations, you can write replies, use relevant media, and record your own voice notes where the chat allows it. Those are ways to explain something personally, not substitutes for a reason to contact someone.

## Email is a present-day difference

ReacherX does not currently send Gmail or Outlook email. A future feature should not be part of the decision if you need it this week.

Similarly, finding a person's email during enrichment doesn't mean ReacherX has an email campaign system ready to send through it. Contact information and sending capability are separate things.

If social activity gives you the context for an introduction, try that job in ReacherX. If cold email is the job, evaluate an email product against its actual sending and reply workflow.

## Account for the work of running software

For either project, self-hosting means infrastructure, external services, and ongoing costs; a free code license doesn't make every API call free. ReacherX also offers a hosted app if you'd rather skip that setup, and either way you should test one complete workflow before relying on it.
