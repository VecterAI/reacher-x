I am a developer and designer myself. I'm trying to build a tool that is powerful yet simple enough that a normal person can use it without having to learn sales or have expertise in marketing.

That's where I'm coming from with ReacherX. Apollo is one of the products people might compare it with, but I don't think of this as trying to build the same thing with a different interface.

## The person you need isn't always a customer

The problem of finding the right people and getting access to them is not just a problem focused on sales. This comes up in almost everything. You want to hire the right people, raise funds, find someone to interview, or start a community. You need a way to find those people and talk to them.

I personally believe ReacherX is a useful kind of tool for small and early teams. Three or four people who are just starting out and need to find the people they're looking for. It's also for a one-person team. Since ReacherX is still pretty early, I don't think it's ready for bigger teams and organizations yet.

A hiring project has different requirements from a customer search. ReacherX gives each project a workspace with its own people, plans, and saved instructions. The use case changes △ Agent's context too. For hiring, you have stages like Interviewing and Hired. You can read more about that in the [CRM guide](/blog/manage-people-with-reacherx).

## What you can do in each product

Apollo's [AI Assistant](https://www.apollo.io/ai/assistant) takes written requests for finding contacts, researching them, and preparing sales outreach. Its [engagement documentation](https://knowledge.apollo.io/hc/en-us/articles/43614439541133-Engage-Prospects-with-the-AI-Assistant) explains how to create sequences with email, calls, and LinkedIn tasks. A sequence is a series of contact attempts, such as an email followed by a reminder to call.

| What you need                                                           | Apollo                                             | ReacherX v4 beta                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Give the assistant written instructions                                 | Yes                                                | Yes                                                                 |
| Run email and calling outreach                                          | Included in its documented sales workflow          | Email sending is planned; current outreach is on X/Twitter and LinkedIn     |
| Find candidates, investors, research participants, or community members | Its documented assistant workflow focuses on sales | Dedicated use cases with different instructions and progress labels |
| Change the product's code                                               | Check Apollo's product and integration options     | Apache-2.0 source available to run and modify                       |

If email or calling is essential for you, that answers a lot of the question already. ReacherX can't currently run your email campaign. Gmail and Outlook sending and calendar integrations are on the roadmap.

## Why I want the conversation inside ReacherX

ReacherX has become a mix of several things: finding people, checking whether they're relevant, gathering background, and managing the people you've found. Social features are part of it too. You can open a profile, read their supported X/Twitter or LinkedIn conversation, and work on what to say from there.

You can [ask for separate plans for several people](/blog/create-plans-for-several-people), discuss changes with △ Agent, and use images, videos, or your own voice notes where supported. I want a normal person to be able to do this without setting up a collection of tools first.

I haven't run a performance benchmark against Apollo. Try a real audience you understand and read the results before deciding. Both products need enough context to know what you mean.
